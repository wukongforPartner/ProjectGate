# Heliocentric

ProjectGate exists to make AI collaboration orbit verifiable facts instead of fluent guesses, short-term convenience, or external metrics.

“Heliocentric” is not a feature name. It is the reason ProjectGate exists.

## Why this name

Most AI collaboration today puts the output at the center.

If an answer looks complete, code appears runnable, or prose sounds fluent, the interaction is often treated as successful. Complex projects do not work that way. Long-term reliability depends on whether the output is anchored to facts, stage boundaries, evidence, and gates that prevent bad assumptions from entering the next iteration.

ProjectGate takes the opposite position:

AI work should not orbit the model’s fluency. It should orbit verified facts.

That is the meaning of Heliocentric.

## The problem is not that AI makes mistakes

AI mistakes are common. The deeper danger is that an unchecked mistake can be reused as a premise, wrapped into later reasoning, and eventually become part of the project structure.

In simple tasks, humans can often patch these errors after the fact.

In long-running engineering work, large codebases, multi-stage tasks, and repeated AI collaboration, errors compound. They enter design, patches, tests, documentation, releases, and future decisions.

ProjectGate is built to fight that compounding failure mode.

It focuses on:

- unverified facts being treated as facts;
- missing context being filled with guesses;
- stage boundaries being skipped;
- known error classes recurring silently;
- failures not becoming reusable rules;
- human authority being blurred;
- fluent work slowly turning into unaccountable uncertainty.

## The core assumption

ProjectGate is built on one engineering assumption:

complex AI collaboration must orbit verifiable facts, or it will eventually collapse into fluent but unaccountable uncertainty.

ProjectGate therefore places these questions at the center:

- What facts are confirmed?
- What facts are still unknown?
- What entries, anchors, states, and files may be affected?
- Is the current stage allowed to proceed?
- Did the output pass the required gate?
- Was failure captured as reusable knowledge?
- Does the human owner retain final judgment and authorization?

## What ProjectGate does not promise

ProjectGate does not promise that AI will never be wrong.

It does not promise that every task will be faster. It does not promise that casual users need it. It does not promise that any model becomes reliable merely by being connected to it.

ProjectGate promises something narrower and stronger:

inside a controlled workflow, facts, stages, gates, risks, and responsibility boundaries must be explicit. Unverified claims cannot masquerade as verified facts. Outputs that fail gates cannot pretend to be complete. Known error classes cannot pass silently.

ProjectGate is not an oracle.

It is a reality layer for AI collaboration.

## Why publish it

ProjectGate was not born for mass-market growth, fundraising, enterprise compromise, or lightweight adoption.

It was first built for long-running, high-friction project work.

Publishing it is not an attempt to persuade everyone.

It is a way to leave behind a complete engineering sample:

what AI collaboration can look like when it is organized around fact locks, stage state, gate validation, and error memory instead of prompt text, emotional reminders, and manual cleanup.

ProjectGate does not need to become lighter for everyone.

It needs to remain complete for those who actually need reliable long-term AI collaboration.

## Heliocentric and ProjectGate

ProjectGate is the technical name: gates, states, checks, constraints, evidence, and collaboration boundaries.

Heliocentric is the principle: AI work should orbit verifiable facts, not fluent output.

Facts are the center.  
AI is the tool.  
Workflow is the orbit.  
Gates are gravity.  
Humans retain final judgment.

That is ProjectGate’s Heliocentric stance.
